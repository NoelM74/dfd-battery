A buyer's guide

How to choose an LFP cell supplier.

There are three realistic ways to buy large-format lithium iron phosphate cells: a tier-1 gigafactory, a marketplace reseller, or direct from a mid-size manufacturer. This page sets out what each one costs you, and which one fits your volume. It includes the cases where we are the wrong answer.

The short answer

If you buy at gigawatt scale, buy from a tier-1 gigafactory. CATL, EVE, Gotion and REPT will beat everyone on cost per kWh at automotive volume, and their certification portfolios cover every market.

If you need a handful of sample cells with no engineering support, a marketplace reseller is faster and cheaper to transact with.

If you sit in between — you need automotive-grade cells, documentation that survives due diligence, and a warranty that will still hold in ten years, at volumes measured in megawatt-hours — buying direct from a mid-size manufacturer is usually the better trade. That is the buyer DFD New Energy is built for.

Common questions

What buyers ask us first.

What are the alternatives to CATL, EVE or Gotion for a sub-gigawatt project?

Buyers who need automotive-grade LFP cells but do not order at gigawatt scale generally have two realistic alternatives to a tier-1 gigafactory: a marketplace reseller, or a mid-size manufacturer selling direct. A reseller is fast and escrow-protected but cannot offer batch traceability, a factory audit, or a warranty it can honour a decade out. A direct manufacturer such as DFD New Energy can offer certificates matched to your production batch, engineering access, OEM and ODM customisation, and a warranty carried by a publicly listed parent company.

Is a tier-1 gigafactory always the safest choice?

For a gigawatt-scale automotive programme, usually yes: the cost per kWh is the lowest available and the certification portfolio covers every market. The trade-off appears below that scale. Smaller accounts are scheduled behind the vehicle OEMs, are served by an account manager rather than a cell engineer, are offered catalogue cells with no pack-level customisation, and are the first to feel an allocation squeeze when supply tightens.

How do I check that a battery manufacturer will still exist in ten years?

Ask three questions. Is the company or its parent publicly listed, so its accounts are auditable? Can you visit and audit the production line before placing a first order? Will the certificates you receive be traceable to your specific production batch, rather than generic type-approval documents? A supplier that cannot answer all three cannot meaningfully warrant a ten-year asset.

What does non-China-origin supply mean and why does it matter?

Tariffs on batteries entering the United States and several other Western markets are assessed on country of origin rather than the seller's nationality. Cells produced outside China can therefore carry a materially different landed cost and a different political profile in sovereign tenders. DFD holds production interests in Indonesia and Bolivia, giving qualifying buyers a non-China-origin route with the same engineering and certifications behind it.

When is DFD New Energy the wrong supplier?

If you order at gigawatt scale, a tier-1 will beat us on cost per kWh and you should buy from them. If you need a handful of sample cells with no engineering support, a marketplace reseller will be faster and cheaper to transact with. We are built for the buyers in between: those who need automotive-grade cells, verifiable documentation and a warranty that will hold, at volumes measured in megawatt-hours rather than gigawatt-hours.
